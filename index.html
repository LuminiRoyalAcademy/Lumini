Welcome to Our 2025 Project

For our Tech Trek project, we chose to reinvent the telegraph — one of the earliest communication devices that changed human history. The telegraph used electrical pulses and Morse code to send messages across long distances, laying the foundation for global connectivity.

We believe in a holistic approach to learning, combining rigorous academics with practical, hands-on experience.

Welcome to Our 2026 Project

For this year's Tech Trek, we're bringing 17th-century Bhutan to life as a first-person action game. Play as Rigpa and the Sky Priests, holding the mountain passes against Tibetan incursions and defending historical Dzongs — the same buildings you can visit today.

Same mission as last year: take something old, understand it deeply, and rebuild it so people can experience it. This time the medium is a game, and the "invention" is a slice of our country's own history.

Why we chose this project

Efficiency

The telegraph could make communication easier without the use of cellphones.

Accessibility

Silent alerts are perfect for accessibility, such as for hearing-impaired users, or for new forms of personal expression.

Emergency Use

It's a great tool for emergency communication in areas without internet or cellular service.

Past Meets Future

Our project is a direct bridge between history and modern innovation. We didn't just rebuild the telegraph; we gave it new life.

This isn't just about nostalgia — it's about proving that a simple, reliable idea, when combined with modern technology, can become a powerful, low-cost, and resilient communication tool for today.

A Country's History, Made Playable

Drugyel is our attempt to take a real chapter of Bhutan's 17th century — the unification, the Dzongs, the arrival of Jesuit priests — and let people actually live inside it for a while.

Every run teaches something: architecture, weapons, language. It's still the same mission as our telegraph — reinvent the old so people can meet it today.

Our Project Journey

From an old invention to a modern solution, here's a look at our process, learnings, and challenges.

Introduction

Our project focused on taking an old invention and reinventing it with modern technology. The goal was to preserve the essence of the original invention while enhancing its functionality, efficiency, and usability through the use of modern tools and methods.

Learning Process

Throughout this journey, we explored and learned multiple platforms that supported our project development:

  • Tinkercad: Helped us understand 3D modeling, prototyping, and visualizing our design.
  • Wokwi: Allowed us to simulate electronic circuits in detail and test our ideas virtually before implementation.
  • Circuit Building: We developed the fundamental skills to design and assemble circuits, which was crucial for bringing our concept to life.

Research

In addition to technical learning, we spent time researching old inventions and their underlying ideologies. This research not only inspired us but also gave us valuable insights into how inventors approached problem-solving in the past. By combining those ideologies with modern technologies, we worked on designing a product that feels both innovative and practical.

Challenges Faced

Our journey was not without difficulties:

  • Time Management: Balancing multiple tasks with tight deadlines proved challenging.
  • Workload: Handling academic responsibilities alongside the project required careful planning.
  • Circuit Building: Since none of us had prior experience, building and testing circuits was a steep learning curve.
  • Using Simulation Tools: Platforms like Wokwi were initially hard to navigate, but with persistence we adapted and improved.

Progress and Achievements

Despite the obstacles, we have steadily moved closer to completing our project. Each milestone achieved has given us a sense of satisfaction and motivation. We are proud of the skills we have developed, from technical expertise to problem-solving and teamwork.

Conclusion

This project has been a valuable journey of learning, experimentation, and perseverance. While we started with little knowledge, we have grown confident in our ability to reinvent an old invention using modern technology. We remain determined to complete and refine our project, with the ultimate aim of not only succeeding in this challenge but also contributing a meaningful innovation.

Circuit Design

We designed a simple circuit for our Smart Telegraph using:

  • ESP32
  • OLED Display
  • Button x3
  • Buzzer
  • Wires
  • Breadboard
  • Resistor
  • USB Power bank

Detailed Function of the Materials:

  • ESP32: Our microcontroller. Works via built-in Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, low power.
  • OLED Display: Displays the letters formed by the entered morse codes.
  • Buttons: Used for entering dots, dashes, and space.
  • Buzzer: Makes the sounds of the dot, dash, and space.
  • Power Bank: Powers the ESP32 and components — makes the device portable.

Learn Morse Code

What is Morse Code?

Morse code transmits information as a series of on-off tones, lights, or clicks. Here a "short tap" is a dit (·) and a "long tap" (or hold) is a dah (–).

The key isn't just the taps — it's the silence in between. Pauses separate letters and words.

The 5 Elements of Morse Timing
  • Dit (·): The base unit of time — a quick tap.
  • Dah (–): Three times as long — holding the button.
  • Intra-character space: Pause between parts of the same letter (~1 dit).
  • Inter-character space: Pause between letters (~3 dits).
  • Word space: Pause between words (~7 dits) — long pause sends the message.

How to Practice

Start with simple letters — E (·), T (–), I (··), M (– –). Then combine into words like "IT" or "ME".

The Animated Emoji "Magic Trick"

Our Morse map isn't just letters — it also maps to internal keywords like EMOJI_WINK. The receiver spots the keyword and runs a special animation function instead of printing text.

Sender side:
  • Tap the special code (e.g. .-.-. for Wink).
  • Lookup finds "EMOJI_WINK".
  • Display shows [WINK].
  • Full raw message with the hidden keyword is sent (e.g. "HI EMOJI_WINK").
Receiver side:
  • Device receives "HI EMOJI_WINK".
  • Loop checks: if (receivedMessage == "EMOJI_WINK").
  • Instead of printing text, calls playWinkAnimation().
  • Animation plays (o o) / (- -) on screen.

What You'll Build (Quick View)

  • Two identical handheld units.
  • Morse Key (1 button) for dots/dashes.
  • Buzzer for audible feedback.
  • OLED screen that shows decoded text.
  • Units talk wirelessly (ESP-WIFI on ESP32) — no router needed.
  • Demo "old vs new": tap Morse → receiver shows live text.

Bill of Materials (per unit)

Two sets needed to build both handheld units.

  • ESP32 DevKit (×1) — DOIT or WROOM-32, built-in Wi-Fi.
  • 0.96" I²C OLED (×1) — 128×64 SSD1306, 4 pins (VCC/GND/SDA/SCL).
  • Momentary Pushbutton (×1) — 12mm NO pushbutton works well.
  • Active Buzzer (×1) — 3.3V or 5V.
  • Breadboard (×1) — 400 or 830 point.
  • Jumper Wires — M-M and M-F.
  • Power Source (×1) — USB power bank or 5V charger.

Morse Timing

  • Dit (•) = 120 ms (~10–12 WPM)
  • Dash (—) ≈ 3× dit
  • Gap between symbols = 1× dit
  • Gap between letters = 3× dit
  • Gap between words = 7× dit

Enclosure

  • Top panel cutouts: OLED (28×14 mm)
  • Internal pillars (M2.5 standoffs)
  • Dimensions: 110×65×30 mm
  • Front label: "Smart Telegraph — Unit A/B"

Build Steps

  1. Breadboard one unit and test the OLED, button, and buzzer.
  2. Flash code with ROLE = SENDER (Unit A) and again with ROLE = RECEIVER (Unit B).
  3. Power both — tap on A → text appears on B; B's buzzer beeps per character.
  4. Second pass: tidy wiring, move to perfboard, or mount in a case.

History: The Lost Invention

1. Lost Invention: The Electrical Telegraph

In the 19th century, the Electrical Telegraph was the pinnacle of human ingenuity — reducing distance to mere seconds using magnets, simple circuits, and a code of taps.

It was "lost" not because it failed, but because it was too fragile and expensive to scale.

The Historical Failure Point (The Gap)

Massive Infrastructure: Miles of dedicated heavy copper wire and bulky mechanical relays — immobile and vulnerable to weather.

Power Inefficiency: Constant high-power current to move physical mechanisms — huge maintenance costs.

Lack of Portability: Communication confined to dedicated, expensive stations.

Conclusion

The design's brilliance was its simple encoding (Morse Code) — 1800s materials and power systems were just too primitive to make it truly resilient and portable.

2. The Modern Solution: LUMINI's Technical Fix

LUMINI revives the Telegraph's core strength — its elegant simplicity — by replacing heavy 19th-century mechanics with 21st-century micro-electronics. Modern tech (all available at a FabLab) directly addresses the historical weaknesses:

Historical Problem
Modern Solution (LUMINI)
Technological Application
Bulky Mechanical Relays
Microcontroller Core (ESP32)
ESP32 reads the physical key-press, digitally encodes the signal — no mechanical switches.
Vulnerable Copper Wiring
Wireless & Low-Power (Wi-Fi)
Low-power Wi-Fi transmits the data wirelessly instead of costly, fragile copper lines.
Expensive, Immobile Housing
Precision Prototyping (3D Printing)
Fusion 360 + 3D printing = compact, durable, lightweight, easily replicated enclosure.

3. How LUMINI Helps (Potential Applications)

Use: Self-contained, off-grid communication tool for disaster zones.

Benefit: When cellular and power grids fail, LUMINI's low-power system and wireless link provide a vital, continuous text-message connection.

Use: Communication in remote areas.

Benefit: Stable, affordable, easily maintainable communication link — built from cheap, globally available components.

Use: Project-based learning.

Benefit: Students learn electromagnetism, digital logic, and circuit design by building a working, historically relevant device.
